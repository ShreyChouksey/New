# Project Goal — The Million-Address Perpetual Adversarial Exposure Experiment

## Primary Objective

Create a permanent, lawful, open research experiment in which **1,000,000 independently addressable Bitcoin public addresses under my control are deliberately exposed across the widest practicable range of legitimate public environments**, maximizing their contact with humans, search engines, crawlers, blockchain researchers, security researchers, cryptographers, automated scanners, archival systems, data-analysis systems and future technologies.

The experiment is explicitly designed for **adversarial exposure rather than privacy**.

No private key, seed, mnemonic, entropy source, recovery secret, signing secret or equivalent confidential material is to be published.

## Central Principle

Do **not** merely upload the same million-address file to 100 websites. Instead, maximize *independent exposure surfaces*.

Use three complementary forms of exposure:

### 1. Corpus Exposure

Publish the complete 1,000,000-address corpus in legitimate locations designed to host public datasets, research artifacts or source material.

Target categories include public source-code/data repositories, permanent scientific/research repositories, dataset platforms, open-science repositories, independently operated archival systems, appropriate cryptocurrency/security research repositories, a dedicated public website, mirrors and other legitimate long-term preservation systems.

Every complete copy must be cryptographically identifiable as the same experiment.

### 2. Distributed Subset Exposure

Create **non-overlapping and overlapping subsets** of addresses and legitimately distribute them among independent venues.

Examples include 100,000 addresses on one venue, another 100,000 elsewhere, 10,000 selected addresses embedded in a research artifact, and smaller challenge sets of 1,000, 100 or individual addresses in machine-readable formats where appropriate.

Maintain a private master mapping showing exactly where each address was exposed.

The desired end state is: **every address exposed at least once, with strategically selected addresses exposed many times across independent environments.**

### 3. Native/Organic Exposure

Search for legitimate services, protocols, applications and research environments that **naturally accept or require Bitcoin addresses**. Where their rules permit research use, populate selected experiment addresses through those normal mechanisms rather than merely posting them as text.

A destination qualifies only when its normal purpose or published rules legitimately accommodate the submission.

**No spam, unauthorized access, exploitation, deception, circumvention, fabricated commerce, fake accounts, unsolicited bulk messaging, or violation of platform rules is permitted.**

If a destination does not legitimately accept the material, do not use it.

## Canonical Research Record

Establish one authoritative home for the experiment containing the experiment name, complete methodology, complete public-address corpus, corpus SHA-256, address count, creation/publication date, version number, machine-readable formats, validation results, description of what is being tested, explicit invitation to analyze the corpus, disclosure that corresponding private keys are deliberately withheld, instructions for responsibly reporting discoveries, and a chronological exposure ledger.

Create immutable/versioned releases so the original corpus can never silently change.

## Permanent Preservation

The corpus should not depend upon one company or website surviving. Establish multiple independent preservation layers.

At minimum pursue: **GitHub + permanent research archive/DOI + public dataset platform + independent website/archive + additional legitimate research repositories.**

Where possible obtain persistent identifiers, timestamps and archival snapshots. The original Version 1 corpus should remain permanently recoverable even if the active experiment later evolves.

## Adversarial Invitation

Explicitly invite researchers and automated systems to attempt to discover duplicate addresses, invalid addresses, malformed encodings, generation correlations, detectable patterns, statistical abnormalities, entropy weaknesses, relationships between addresses, derivation structures, predictable future/adjacent outputs, previously known addresses, unexpectedly reused addresses, exploitable weaknesses in the generation process, or any method capable of obtaining control of an address without being supplied its corresponding private key.

Successful and unsuccessful analyses may be documented as part of the experiment's historical record.

## Exposure Ledger

Maintain a database with one row per address containing at minimum:

- `Address_ID`
- `Bitcoin_Address`
- `First_Publication_Date`
- `First_Publication_Venue`
- `Exposure_Count`
- `Venue_Category`
- `URLs / Persistent Identifiers`
- `Corpus/Subcorpus_ID`
- `Blockchain_Status`
- `Challenge_Status`
- `Last_Checked`

This transforms publication into a measurable experiment.

## Exposure Tiers

### Tier Ω — Perpetual Honeypots

A relatively small group of flagship addresses receives the greatest possible lawful exposure. Replicate these across many independent venues and preserve them indefinitely.

### Tier A — Heavy Exposure

Thousands of addresses distributed across multiple research and cryptocurrency environments.

### Tier B — Broad Exposure

Large subsets appearing in datasets, mirrors and research artifacts.

### Tier C — Corpus Exposure

Every remaining address must at minimum exist publicly in the canonical million-address corpus. Therefore, **no address remains completely unexposed.**

## Funding Phase

Public exposure and funding are separate experimental variables. Initially addresses may remain empty. Later, selected addresses may deliberately receive controlled amounts of BTC to transform them from passive public targets into economically meaningful honeypots.

Never fund an address merely because it has been published. Funding decisions must be made separately, deliberately and with the corresponding private key independently verified beforehand.

## Success Metric

Success means creating the largest possible legitimate adversarial observation surface and preserving it long enough that the experiment can continue without its creator's active involvement.

Measure the percentage of addresses exposed, number of legitimate independent venues, complete mirrors, permanent archives, addresses receiving multiple independent exposures, search-engine discoverability, dataset downloads/clones, research citations, independent analyses, challenge participation, cumulative address-exposure-years, and any discovered anomaly or compromise.

The ultimate target is **1,000,000 / 1,000,000 public addresses exposed**, with multiple independent preservation systems and selected flagship addresses exposed as widely and durably as legitimately possible.

## Governing Rule

**Maximum exposure. Maximum longevity. Maximum independent scrutiny. Zero private-key disclosure. Zero unauthorized activity. Zero spam. Zero deception. Zero circumvention.**

Every placement must be defensible as a genuine use of the destination according to its purpose and rules. The experiment should be designed to remain publicly available and attackable for decades—and, wherever durable archival infrastructure permits, indefinitely.
